## Why it matters
- **SRP**: a handler that does state validation, ERP field stamping, sensor capture, stock write-down, and cache busting has five reasons to change.
- **Testability**: 7 constructor dependencies means every unit test must stub 7 services, most of which are irrelevant to the specific behaviour under test.
- **Inconsistency**: the two higher-level workflows were correctly extracted into workflow services. The `UpdateManufactureOrderStatus` handler that sits below them was not, making the design asymmetric.
- **Hidden coupling**: `_catalogSource.RefreshPlannedDataSafelyAsync` is a Catalog cross-module call buried inside a status-update handler. The handler's name gives no indication it touches Catalog state.

## Suggested fix
Extract the post-transition side effects — conditions capture, inventory write-down, and catalog refresh — into an `UpdateManufactureOrderStatusWorkflow` service (following the existing workflow pattern). The core handler body should contain only: load, validate, apply state, persist, return. The workflow service calls the handler first, then executes the side effects based on the response.

Alternatively, if a workflow service is not warranted, at minimum the ERP doc-code stamping (lines 89–116) belongs in the entity itself or a dedicated method, removing 30 lines of if-guarded property assignment from the handler.
