## Finding
Both `CreateManufactureAsync` and `DisassembleGiftPackageAsync` in `GiftPackageManufactureService` (Application/Features/Logistics/UseCases/GiftPackageManufacture/Services/GiftPackageManufactureService.cs) split their work across two database transactions:

**CreateManufactureAsync (lines 150–207):**
1. Creates `GiftPackageManufactureLog`, then calls `_giftPackageRepository.SaveChangesAsync` — log is committed (line 158–159).
2. Calls `_stockOperationService.CreateOperationAsync` once per ingredient + once for the output product — these are separate operations after the commit.

**DisassembleGiftPackageAsync (lines 210–300):**  
Same pattern: log committed at lines 239–240, stock operations follow in a loop.

`GiftPackageManufactureLog` has no `Status` or `IsComplete` field. If any `CreateOperationAsync` call throws after the log is saved, the log row persists in the database but the corresponding stock movements are absent or incomplete. There is no compensating transaction, no saga/outbox pattern, and no mechanism to detect and re-drive partial executions.

The comment "CRITICAL: Save the log FIRST to get the ID for DocumentNumber" (lines 156, 237) acknowledges the coupling: the document-number scheme `GPM-{logId:000000}-{productCode}` creates a dependency on the database-generated ID.

## Why it matters
Partial failures leave the inventory in an inconsistent state: the log says N packages were manufactured, but the catalog stock counters reflect fewer operations. This is a silent data integrity bug — it does not throw to the caller, and there is no background reconciliation job visible in the codebase.

## Suggested fix
Two viable options:

**Option A — Wrap in a single ambient transaction:**  
Use `IDbContextTransaction` to open a transaction before the first `SaveChangesAsync` and commit only after all stock operations succeed. The log and all stock operations are committed atomically. The document-number scheme still works because EF Core generates the ID within the transaction before commit.

**Option B — Add a status field to `GiftPackageManufactureLog`:**  
Mark the log as `Pending` on creation and `Completed` after all stock operations succeed. A background reconciliation job retries `Pending` entries older than N minutes. This is the outbox pattern — more complex but resilient to infrastructure failures that a transaction boundary cannot catch (e.g., the process dying mid-loop).

Option A is the minimal change; Option B is required if `CreateOperationAsync` calls an external service that cannot participate in the database transaction.
